abstract: Solar radio bursts exhibit complex fine structures that reveal intricate
  coronal plasma dynamics. Here, we report detection of spike-like repeating burst
  pairs, characterized by two short-lived (0.1-2 s), narrowband components separated
  by about 4 s at frequencies 30-50 MHz. Using high-resolution dynamic spectra and
  spectroscopic imaging, we analyzed 613 burst pairs, measuring their durations, bandwidths,
  drift rates, flux densities, and spatial characteristics. Imaging links sources
  to an active region, with earlier components spatially concentrated above the region
  while delayed components are displaced and exhibit reduced drift rates. Radio-wave
  propagation simulations support the delayed bursts as turbulent echoes of harmonic
  emission in anisotropic coronal plasma. The location of the burst sources high in
  the corona suggests ongoing magnetic reconnection and electron acceleration well
  above typical flare heights. Our findings offer new insights into coronal turbulence
  effects while advancing diagnostics of coronal plasma and the elusive nature of
  solar radio echoes from ground-based transmitters.
