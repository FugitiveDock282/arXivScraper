abstract: The X-ray source in AGN is commonly referred to as the corona by analogy
  to stellar coronae. The similarities between the two suggest that the heating mechanism
  of AGN coronae is magnetic reconnection -- as in cool stars -- but this has not
  yet been directly observed. This work presents the first observational evidence
  for a magnetic reconnection flare in an AGN corona. We report on a flare in NGC
  3783, which was observed with XRISM/Xtend and XMM-Newton/EPIC-PN exhibiting distinct
  temporal evolution in soft ( $<2.0\,$ keV) and hard ( $>2.0\,$ keV) X-rays. An Ultra-Fast
  Outflow (UFO) was detected during the event. The flare features the Neupert effect
  -- a temporal signature widely observed in the Sun, which shows that the flare is
  powered by magnetic reconnection, with the UFO playing a role analogous to a solar
  Coronal Mass Ejection (CME). We derive an upper limit of $30 \, R_g$ on the height
  of the magnetic loop from which the flare originates. Using the UFOs measured properties
  to characterize the magnetic field, we obtain $B > 1.3 \times 10^4\,$ G for the
  field annihilated during the flare from total energy considerations, and $B \approx
  500\,$ G for the momentary magnetic field during reconnection from a dynamical consideration.
