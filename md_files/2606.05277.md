abstract: The formation of gravitationally bound overdensities of dark matter (DM),
  or \textit{halos}, is a generic prediction of theories with particle DM. We present
  a measurement of halo properties in 28 quadruple image strong lens systems recently
  observed by JWST, and use these observations to constrain the free-streaming length,
  $λ_{\rm{FS}}$ , of DM, a quantity that depends on the DM particle mass and formation
  mechanism. We improve on previous lensing analyses by simultaneously reconstructing
  extended lensed arcs with image positions and relative magnifications, enhancing
  sensitivity to perturbations by halos. Our analysis rules out deviations from the
  predictions of cold dark matter (CDM) on scales above $10^{7.2} M_{\odot}$ and $10^{7.4}
  M_{\odot}$ for subhalo abundance predicted by cosmological $N$ -body simulations
  and semi-analytic models, respectively. These bounds correspond to upper limits
  $λ_{\rm{FS}}<6.0 \ \rm{kpc}$ and $λ_{\rm{FS}}<7.0 \ \rm{kpc}$ , and lower limits
  on the mass of a spin--1/2 thermal relic DM particle $m_{\rm{therm}}>7.4 \ \rm{keV}$
  and $m_{\rm{therm}}>6.5 \ \rm{keV}$ . Conversely, assuming a negligible free-streaming
  length, as predicted by CDM, we measure a projected mass in subhalos around elliptical
  galaxies $1.7_{-1.2}^{+2.6} \times 10^7 \ \mathrm{M}_{\odot} \ \rm{kpc^{-2}}$ at
  $95 \%$ confidence. These inferences confirm key predictions of the CDM paradigm.
