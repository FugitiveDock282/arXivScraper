abstract: Accretion onto strongly magnetized neutron stars is commonly interpreted
  using quasi-steady models, in which the accretion-column structure adjusts smoothly
  to the mass inflow rate. The cyclotron line in the X-ray spectrum, whose centroid
  energy traces the magnetic field strength and thus the height of the line-forming
  region, provides a key diagnostic of this structure. Whether this simple quasi-steady
  description remains valid on short dynamical timescales has remained uncertain.
  Here we show that, during a giant outburst of the X-ray pulsar 1A~0535+262, quasi-periodic
  hard X-ray flux variations are accompanied by synchronized oscillations of the cyclotron
  line energy, with amplitudes exceeding those expected from simple accretion-rate
  fluctuations. The anti-correlation between cyclotron energy and apparent flux provides
  direct spectral-timing evidence for rapid changes in the line-forming region, which
  we interpret as geometric reconfiguration of the accretion column. The variability
  emerges in the luminosity regime where radiation pressure becomes dynamically important.
  These results reveal limitations of a simple quasi-steady interpretation for this
  source and suggest that radiation-supported columns can enter intrinsically dynamical
  states in high-luminosity accreting pulsars.
