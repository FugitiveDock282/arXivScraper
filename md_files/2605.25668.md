abstract: Sealed neutron tubes have a wide range of applications, and the ion source
  is their core component. Penning ion sources commonly suffer from issues such as
  uneven magnetic field distribution and a low proportion of monoatomic ions. Improving
  the performance of the ion source can effectively address the problems of low neutron
  flux and short operational lifespan. This study aims to optimise the magnetic field
  configuration and discharge parameters of the ion source, thereby increasing the
  proportion of monoatomic and enhancing discharge stability, and to provide a design
  basis for high-performance sealed neutron tubes. Develop a magnetic field-plasma
  coupling model to compare and analyze the magnetic field distribution patterns of
  traditional magnetic block structures and soft iron-reinforced structures, and investigate
  the mechanisms by which operating pressure and anode voltage affect plasma density
  and ion composition using COMSOL multiphysics simulation methods. Simulation results
  indicate that the soft iron structure significantly enhances the axial magnetic
  field strength and uniformity within the discharge region; under conditions of 0.06
  Pa gas pressure and 1500 V anode voltage, the proportion of monoatomic ions increased
  from the conventional 9% to 30%.
