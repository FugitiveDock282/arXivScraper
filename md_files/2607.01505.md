abstract: The magneto-rotational instability can generate strong, turbulent substructure
  within magnetised shear flows. The efficacy of the mechanism as a function of microphysical
  aspects of the fluid, such as stratification and diffusivity, has been explored
  extensively. One aspect that has not been studied thus far, however, is whether
  the instability can also operate in solids. Motivated by the possibility that solid
  regions within planets or degenerate stars may rotate differentially with respect
  to liquid or gaseous layers during some phase of their life, we examine the extent
  to which elasticity suppresses the instability. A simplified, plane-parallel analysis
  reveals that only in cases where the flow is strongly sheared, such that the magnetic
  tension that would result from the instability in a liquid exceeds the shear modulus
  of the elastic cavity, can magnetic growth occur. In the context of dynamical tides
  in binary neutron-star mergers, this implies that the magnetic field can be amplified
  in the crust prior to coalescence only if the star boasts a spin frequency of $\gtrsim
  300$ Hz. If viscous heating weakens the crystalline structure prior to resonance,
  the required spin frequency is reduced.
