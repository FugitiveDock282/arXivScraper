abstract: Optimal transport provides an efficient method to infer the displacement
  of objects by mapping their initial positions to their present-day locations over
  cosmic time; equivalently, it enables the reconstruction of initial positions from
  measurements taken at later times. The method has been shown to be accurate even
  if positions for only a biased subset of the particles are measured, provided that
  the initial displacement field was Gaussian. The method does not rely on the assumption
  of a Gaussian displacement field, and thus may be extended to the reconstruction
  of non-Gaussian initial conditions. Here, we demonstrate how this is achieved for
  a class of `local` primordial non-Gaussian fields of current interest in cosmology.
  For these models, there is a distinctive signature in the large scale clustering
  of biased tracers which depends on the product of the primordial amplitude $f_{\rm
  NL}$ and the nature of the tracers $b_φ$ . Our method exploits the fact that this
  signature is not present in the full field; it is only present in biased fields.
  Therefore, the mass that is not in the biased subset, what we call the `dust`, also
  has a characteristic scale-dependence, albeit of a different amplitude. We show
  that the quality of the optimal transport reconstruction improves as the model for
  this dust becomes more realistic.
