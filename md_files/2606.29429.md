abstract: Energy conversion by reconnection-powered nanoflare heating is one of the
  leading explanations for the heating of the solar chromosphere and corona. The aim
  of this paper is to shed light on this mechanism by exploring the magnetic Reynolds
  number dependence of the energy conversion process. To do this we employ boundary-driven,
  magnetohydrodynamic, flux-braiding simulations at different magnetic Reynolds numbers
  ( $R_m$ ), and explore in detail the properties of the individual magnetic energy
  release events. The properties of the reconnecting current sheets that mediate the
  energy release are shown to depend on $R_m$ . For increasing $R_m$ , the current
  sheets become thinner, more intense, and more numerous. For sufficiently large $R_m$
  , the current sheets fragment along their length, leading to a sharp cutoff in the
  current sheet length distribution. The cutoff is consistent with the threshold for
  non-linear tearing/plasmoid instability. For increasing $R_m$ the magnetic field
  lines become increasingly tangled, the mean and peak values of the magnetic field
  strength increase, and the Poynting flux into the domain increases, implying that
  the heating rate also increases. The global reconnection rate is essentially independent
  of $R_m$ . These results support the braiding mechanism as a viable way to effectively
  heat the internal portions of coherent flux tubes in the corona.
