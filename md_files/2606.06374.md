abstract: We study the motion of columnar ice crystals that form in clouds over a
  range of low temperature. Our focus here is on elongated ice crystals, which are
  smaller than the size of the smallest eddies in the flow, with a moderate aspect
  ratio comprised between $3$ and $5$ . We determine turbulent solutions of the Navier-Stokes
  equations over a range of turbulent kinetic energy dissipation characteristic of
  clouds ( $4.41\;{\rm cm}^2/{\rm s}^3 \le \varepsilon \le 1120\;{\rm cm}^2/{\rm s}^3$
  ) by using direct numerical simulations, and we follow the motion of crystals using
  simplified but realistic models for the motion of non-spherical, elongated particles.
  The influence of the fluid inertia leads to a preferential alignment of the crystals
  perpendicular to the direction of gravity, the alignment effect being opposed by
  the turbulent fluctuations. Along with the strong alignment of the crystal axis
  perpendicular to gravity, we observe only a weak alignment with the vorticity, much
  weaker than in the absence of gravity. The settling velocity depends only weakly
  on the orientation of the crystals, but is strongly enhanced when $\varepsilon$
  increases, an effect that we attribute to preferential concentration in the flow.
  As the inertia of the columnar ice crystals considered here is significant, we observe
  a strong spatial clustering. Finally, we discuss the relevance of the effects identified
  here on the collision frequency between ice crystals in cloud conditions.
